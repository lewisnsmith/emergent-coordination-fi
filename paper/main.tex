\begin{abstract}
% One paragraph: question, method (replay + shared exchange, matched
% baselines, pre-registered inference), headline result, implication.
Foundation models may become a common decision layer across market
participants. We preregister a crossed technology-by-ecology experiment that
separates LLM versus classical decision rules from homogeneous versus
heterogeneous cohort construction. The primary outcome is common-response
convergence across independent market trajectories and nonoverlapping replay
windows. A secondary, explicitly simulator-bounded experiment tests how
randomized AI capital share changes market outcomes. Numerical results remain
withheld until the verified multi-block release gate passes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Financial agents built on a small number of shared foundation models may react
similarly to common information even when they never communicate. Existing
experimental work already finds that LLM traders can be less behaviorally
dispersed than people and that heterogeneous LLM markets can be unstable
\citep{henning2025llm,saxena2026machine}. The open question is therefore not
whether convergence can occur, but which shared components produce it and
whether it survives a fair comparison with classical decision rules.

We address that question with (i) a crossed technology-by-ecology benchmark,
(ii) matched information, capital, constraints, activity, and action marginals,
(iii) paired single-component interventions, and (iv) inference over independent
market trajectories or nonoverlapping windows. A separate randomized exchange
experiment estimates an AI-capital-share dose response only within the validated
simulator. This design is a replication and causal decomposition, not a claim of
first evidence for LLM herding.

\section{Related work}
Our convergence measures build on institutional-herding statistics
\citep{lakonishok1992impact,sias2004institutional}. The market-design baseline
follows the experimental double-auction tradition and its demonstration that
market institutions can generate allocative regularities even with minimally
rational traders \citep{smith1962experimental,gode1993allocative}.

The closest LLM precedents occupy the broad claims. \citet{henning2025llm}
compare homogeneous and mixed LLM markets with human experiments;
\citet{saxena2026machine} study fifteen models in homogeneous and heterogeneous
markets; \citet{ouyang2026dissecting} causally manipulate prompted behavioral
mechanisms; and \citet{lopezlira2025trade} supplies a persistent-order-book LLM
market. Our incremental contribution is the matched classical control and
factorial component decomposition under independent-unit inference.

\section{Experimental design}
The H1 benchmark crosses technology (LLM or classical) with ecology
(homogeneous or heterogeneous). Homogeneous cells repeat one exact model or
strategy family with seed and parameter variation; heterogeneous cells use
balanced mixtures across families. Family-specific estimates are combined with
frozen equal weights. Every cell shares the opportunity set, forecast horizon,
information, initial capital, trading constraints, and risk limits. Activity,
turnover, and buy/hold/sell marginals are matched or included in prespecified
sensitivities.

H3 and H4 use paired Hamming-distance-one contrasts on identical trajectories.
Model releases are fixed effects, and at least one family is held out from
specification selection. The highest independent unit is a generated trajectory
or nonoverlapping historical window; agents, pairs, steps, prompt paraphrases,
retries, and response seeds are nested observations.

H5 assigns independently initialized whole-market replicas to AI capital shares
of 0, 10, 25, 50, 75, or 100 percent. Its conclusions are causal only under the
simulator's assignment and mechanism. The simulator report must document its
order persistence, reservations, background liquidity, endowments, fees, and
validation against spread, depth, volume, volatility, impact, fill, and no-trade
targets before H5 results are enabled.

\section{Metrics and inference}
Primary H1 outcomes are block-level differences in Cohen's $\kappa$, with
portfolio overlap and validated strategy-fingerprint dispersion as ordered
secondary outcomes. LSV uses the contemporaneous market-wide expected-buy
fraction; Sias demand correlation is decomposed into following one's own prior
demand and following other agents \citep{lakonishok1992impact,sias2004institutional}.
Rationale similarity is exploratory.

The analysis first reduces all nested observations to one contrast per
independent block. Sign-flip tests are labeled as symmetry-based sensitivities
unless they reproduce an actual random assignment. Exact enumeration is used
when feasible; Monte Carlo tests record their seed, draw count, correction, and
attainable minimum $p$-value. Confirmatory families use frozen Holm correction,
and effect intervals are shown against zero and the smallest effect size of
interest. Equivalence requires two one-sided tests; a nonsignificant difference
is never called equivalence.

\section{Robustness and diagnostics}
The required suite includes chance floors, activity-matched estimates,
prevalence-robust agreement, intent-versus-execution contrasts, missingness and
failure analyses, model/provider leave-one-out estimates, held-out-family
replication, and specification curves. For H5 it additionally includes arrival
rules, endowments, tick size, fees, book persistence, background liquidity, and
order-type stress tests. These analyses are mandatory whether they strengthen
or weaken the headline estimate.

\section{Discussion}
Agreement among replay agents that cannot observe one another is common-response
convergence, not coordination or collusion. Strategic language requires
interaction evidence and a profitable joint mechanism. Likewise, an H5 effect
is evidence about the implemented simulator, not real-market systemic impact.
The conclusion will classify every estimand as practically supported,
statistically detectable but small, equivalent or noninferior, or inconclusive.

\section{Limitations}
The population is limited to dated model releases, prompts, constraints,
trajectories, and classical families sampled by the study. Model contamination,
provider drift, prompt sensitivity, a finite classical-strategy menu, long-only
constraints, and simulation fidelity limit external validity. Historical
replay identifies correlated response under a fixed path, not endogenous price
effects. No result establishes human or institutional superiority unless a
separately harmonized external panel passes its data gate.

\section{Ethics and disclosure}
No experiment places a live trade or supplies personalized financial advice.
Provider prompts and logs can contain proprietary model outputs and are released
only as licenses permit. Human-subject protocols remain excluded unless prior
ethics approval and consent are documented. The release reports funding,
conflicts, provider credits, and all model or data access furnished by third
parties.
